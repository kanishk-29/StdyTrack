How to Prepare for Exams: A Step-by-Step Study Plan

A practical, week-by-week revision schedule so you walk into the exam room prepared and calm.

Updated September 2026 · 10 min read

Exam week sneaks up on everyone. The secret to doing well isn’t studying harder in the final days — it’s having a clear plan from the start. Here’s a step-by-step approach that works whether you have two weeks or two months.

Step 1: List Every Exam and Its Date

Write down all your exams, their dates, and how many marks or what percentage each one counts toward your final grade. This is the first thing a good exam tracker helps you do. Prioritise exams that are sooner or worth more.

Step 2: Break the Syllabus Into Small Topics

Big units feel overwhelming. Split each subject into small, individual topics — "Chapter 3: Photosynthesis" is easier to tackle than "Biology". Turn your syllabus into a checklist and tick topics off as you master them.

Tip: aim to cover about one small topic per focused study session. That gives you a realistic sense of how much time you really need.

Step 3: Build a Revision Schedule Backwards From Exam Day

Count the days until the exam and divide your topics across them. Leave the final 2–3 days for full mock tests and light review, not new material.

A simple two-week revision plan

WeekWhat to do
Week 1Cover all topics, one at a time. Make concise notes and flashcards.
Days 1–3 (week 2)Active recall on every topic. Re-do the hardest ones.
Day 4–5 (week 2)Practice with past papers under exam conditions.
Day 6–7Mocks, review mistakes, and lighter revision. Rest well.

Step 4: Study With Active Recall, Not Re-reading

Testing yourself is proven to work better than re-reading notes. After each topic, close your notes and try to recall everything. Use flashcards and practice questions. Re-reading gives you the comfortable illusion of knowing — recall shows the truth.

Step 5: Use Past Papers

Past papers are the single best predictor of exam questions. Do them under timed conditions, then mark them strictly and study every question you got wrong. Repeated questions appear in most subjects.

Step 6: Track Your Study Time Per Subject

Keep a simple log of how many hours you study each subject. If a subject is getting zero hours, that’s a problem you need to fix before the exam. Tracking time turns vague worry into a clear picture. Tools like the Study Tracker make this automatic.

Step 7: Take Breaks and Sleep

Your brain needs rest to store memories. Study in focused blocks with short breaks (try the Pomodoro method), and avoid all-nighters. The night before the exam, sleep is more valuable than one more hour of review.

Step 8: Review Mistakes the Day After Practice

Don’t just mark practice papers and move on. Go back the next day and re-test everything you missed. Fixing mistakes soon after you make them is how you stop them from repeating.

Step 9: Do a Full Mock Exam a Few Days Before

Simulate the real thing: same length, same conditions, no distractions. This trains your timing and reduces exam-day anxiety because the situation is already familiar.

Step 10: Manage Exam-Day Nerves

Sleep well, eat before the exam, arrive early, and do a light 10-minute recall of key formulas or definitions just before walking in. Panic is usually a sign of not having a plan — and you now have one.
