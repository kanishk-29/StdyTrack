Scattered Notes, Forgotten Deadlines, Lost Hours? Here’s the Solution

Study Tracker was built to solve one problem: studying is chaotic, and most students are fighting a system that isn’t built for how they actually learn.

Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

The Problem: Every Student Knows This Feeling

Have you ever found yourself the night before an exam, frantically flipping through a notebook, a phone note, a PDF, and a Google Doc — because your notes are scattered in five different places? Or sat down to "study" and realize two hours later you’ve done almost nothing?

That’s not a lack of intelligence. It’s a lack of organisation. When your study life is scattered, every extra minute you spend looking for a note is a minute not spent learning. The problems are almost always the same:

  • Notes everywhere. Handwritten, typed, screenshots, voice memos — nothing in one place, nothing searchable.
  • No time awareness. You have no idea how many hours actually went into each subject. Easy subjects get over-studied, hard ones get avoided.
  • Forgotten deadlines. Exams creep up because nothing is recorded or prioritised.
  • No feedback. You can’t see progress. Without data, you can’t tell if your study method is working.
  • Easy to lose focus. Without a clear plan and structure, distractions win.

The Solution: One Place for Everything

Study Tracker gives every student a single, organized home for their studies — so you spend your time learning, not searching.

Here’s how it solves each part of the problem:

1. Organize Subjects, Units & Lectures

Structure your work the way your brain does: subject → unit → lecture. Everything lives in a clear hierarchy, so you always know where a note belongs and what you’ve covered.

2. Rich-Text Notes for Every Lecture

Take notes directly on each lecture — with bold, lists, headings, highlights, colors, and even drawings. Use multiple pages per note and search across all of them at once. No more flipping between a notebook and a phone.

3. A Study Timer That Shows Where Your Time Goes

Log how long you actually study each subject. See a breakdown of your hours so you can catch subjects you’ve been quietly avoiding and balance your week. Tracking is the first step to controlling your time.

4. Track Exam Scores & See Progress

Record your test scores and watch your performance improve subject by subject. Seeing progress on a graph is motivating — and shows you what’s working.

5. A Daily View to Stay on Track

Start each day with a clear view of what to study, what’s due, and where your focus should be. Less mental noise, more momentum.

Why It Works

We’re not here to tell you studying is easy. But organisation removes the friction that makes studying harder than it needs to be. When your notes are in the right place, your time is tracked, and your plan is visible, the hard part becomes the learning — not the chaos around it.
