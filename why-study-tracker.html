● STUDY TRACKERI · THE STORY
A personal system, built from a real problem

The hardest part wasn't studying.
It was managing everything worth studying.

College coursework had its deadlines. Personal learning had its own pace. Research kept opening new questions. Team projects brought another set of responsibilities. Each one was worth doing. The problem was that they all wanted the same hours.

I was using separate notes, folders, lists, and small systems to keep everything organised. Eventually I realised something ridiculous: I was spending more time managing the system than using it.

So I stopped looking for the right productivity app. I built the system I wanted to have.

● STUDY TRACKERV · THE CRAFT
THE PART NOBODY SEES

Features get screenshots. This stuff gets ignored — right up until the day it quietly saves you.

01

Two devices walk into a sync conflict

Your phone says one thing. Your laptop says another. Someone has to lose — and there's no committee meeting about it. No merge attempt. No "pick a version" popup making you do the app's job for it. Whoever saved last, wins. That's the whole rule. It's almost rude how simple it is. It's also exactly right.

Study Tracker
02

14 null-guarded CRUD chains

Here's a scenario nobody plans for: you're mid-edit on a lecture, and your other device deletes the subject underneath it — mid-conversation, so to speak. Your modal is now pointing at a ghost. The app is not allowed to panic about this. So all 14 lookup chains check, quietly, every time, whether the thing they're reaching for still exists. Nobody notices this. That's the point.

03

One JSON document is the entire database

No tables. No schemas. Not a single line of SQL anywhere in this system. Every subject, every log, every habit, every setting — one JSON blob, one key. It sounds like a shortcut. It's actually the whole trick: when there's nothing partial, there's nothing to untangle. That's why "newest write wins" gets to be that simple.

04

"Today" is computed, not assumed

Most apps ask the device what day it is, shrug, and trust it. That's usually fine. Usually. Here, "today" is calculated — noon-anchored, timezone-aware, 24 presets deep — so the calendar can't flip at some arbitrary hour just because your phone thinks it's somewhere else. Logs, streaks, the planner, exam pacing — all reading off the same clock.

● STUDY TRACKERV · THE CRAFT (CONTINUED)
05

Offline mode that doesn't rot

You've felt this bug before, even if you didn't know its name: an app updates, but your browser keeps serving yesterday's version anyway. No warning. No error. Just quiet, low-grade confusion. That's a stale cache — fixed here by stripping version numbers before the cache gets a say. New release ships, old cache can't argue back.

06

Cost-aware cloud writes

Not every keystroke deserves its own trip to the cloud — that's a fast way to burn through a free tier by lunchtime. So writes are throttled, capped at once every 15 seconds. But nothing gets left behind: one final write is always guaranteed to land. Cheap and reliable — normally you only get to pick one.

07

Storage that adapts

Drop this thing anywhere and it figures out where to put your data without being asked — host storage if it exists, IndexedDB if it doesn't, localStorage if nothing else is left standing. No config screen. No setup wizard interrogating you about things the app should already know.

THE PRINCIPLE

None of this photographs well. All of it is the actual reason this still feels solid a year in — not just for the first five impressed minutes.
